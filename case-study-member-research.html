Research · Collective Health

Listening in Spanish: what member research shaped Q2–Q3.

As Collective Health expanded Spanish-language support across the member portal, EOBs, and contact center, we ran a round of interviews with Spanish-speaking members to understand whether that investment was actually landing — and where it wasn't.

RoleProduct Designer
FocusMember Experience
TimelineQ1 2025
MethodModerated interviews
A note on this project: the findings below are presented at a thematic level only. No direct quotes, individual participant details, or identifying information from the original research are included, out of respect for participant privacy and the confidentiality of Collective Health's research data.
The question

Is Spanish-language support actually reducing friction for our members — or just checking a box?

Method

Talking directly to the members we built this for

Rather than relying on support-ticket volume or survey scores alone, we ran moderated interviews with Spanish-speaking members, using a bilingual discussion guide so participants could respond in whichever language felt most natural. Sessions covered their broader healthcare experience, first impressions of Collective Health, and hands-on time with the Spanish-language portal and EOBs.

Format
1:1 moderated interviews, bilingual guide
Topics
Onboarding, portal usage, Spanish content, support channels
Output
Observer notes synthesized into themes
Findings

What we heard

What worked
Spanish support felt like a real benefit, not a checkbox. Members who used the Spanish portal, chat, and phone support described it as a genuine improvement over previous insurance experiences — not just a translated version of the same thing.
The glossary reduced confusion. Plain-language definitions for insurance terms (deductibles, copays, etc.) were consistently called out as one of the most useful parts of the portal.
The overall experience compared favorably to prior providers. Members described clearer explanations and shorter wait times than they were used to with previous insurance companies.
Tone landed well. The informal "tú" register used in Spanish content felt approachable to most participants, though it's worth watching how that lands with older members.
What didn't
Verification codes were an early blocker. Delayed or missing email verification codes during registration created unnecessary friction — a technical issue, but one with an outsized impact on first impressions.
Physical mail stayed English-only. Printed communications and ID cards hadn't caught up with the digital experience, which mattered most for members with lower English proficiency.
Behavioral health resources felt limited. Several participants wanted more options for counseling — particularly for children and adolescents — and felt underserved in their local area.
App vs. portal parity. Some members defaulted to the mobile app purely for speed, suggesting the web portal still has ground to make up on convenience.
Impact

From interview notes to roadmap

These findings — together with the rest of the interview round — became direct input into scoping Member Experience work for the following two quarters.

Q2

Extend Spanish-language support to physical mail and ID cards, and reduce delivery delays on email verification codes.

Q3

Explore expanded behavioral health resources for dependents, and close remaining feature gaps between the app and web portal.

Reflection

What this project taught me

Support-ticket data told us Spanish-speaking members were engaging with the portal. It took direct conversations to learn why some parts of that experience still felt incomplete — like mail that never got translated, or a verification flow that undermined trust in the very first minutes of onboarding.

It reinforced something I keep relearning: a translated interface isn't the same thing as a localized experience. The gap between the two is exactly where research earns its place in the roadmap.
